# NetSentry — Conformance Mapping (NIST AI RMF and the EU AI Act)

_NIST AI RMF 1.0 (January 2023); Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, high-risk obligations (Articles 9-15). Every control names an artifact and every artifact is checked to
exist: a study that is deleted or renamed downgrades its own control on the next run. Regenerate
with `netsentry compliance`; the machine-readable mapping is
[`compliance_mapping.json`](compliance_mapping.json)._

## Why this report exists

A detector that decides which traffic a human looks at attracts obligations, and the engineering
question underneath the legal one is answerable: **for each thing a regulator or an internal
model-risk function would ask for, is there an artifact here that satisfies it, and where?**
This maps two frameworks that ask differently — NIST's AI RMF is process-shaped ("do you know
what you built and do you watch it?"), the EU AI Act's high-risk articles are obligation-shaped
("does this specific thing exist?").

Across 26 controls: **19 met**, 4 partial, 2 unmet, 1 not applicable — 93% of the applicable NIST functions and 73% of the applicable EU articles, counting a partial as a half.

Every claim's evidence was found on disk. That sentence is worth exactly as much as the check behind it, which is why the check is in the code rather than in the prose.

## The gaps

The gaps are the useful half of this document, and they share a shape: **the things this repository cannot evidence are organisational rather than technical.**

- **MEASURE 2.11 — Fairness and bias across relevant groups are evaluated** (partial): Per-service detection parity is audited with Wilson intervals and served as a `per_service` threshold profile, and automatic slice discovery searches for underperforming regions nobody predefined. Both are *operational* groupings; this system processes no personal attributes, so demographic fairness is not assessed and no claim about it should be inferred.
- **MANAGE 4.1 — Post-deployment monitoring plans are implemented, including incident response** (partial): Drift monitors, SLO burn-rate alerts and incident reports exist and are wired to Prometheus; what does not exist is an organisational response *plan* — who is paged, what authority they have, and how a rollback is authorised are outside what a repository can evidence.
- **Article 9 — Risk management system across the lifecycle** (partial): Risks are identified, measured and re-measured after mitigation throughout (measure - fix - re-measure appears in hardening, poisoning defence and the control-loop attack). What is missing is the formal artifact: a maintained risk register with owners and residual-risk acceptance, which is a governance document rather than a study.
- **Article 14 — Human oversight: designed so a person can intervene and override** (partial): The system routes rather than acts: conformal sets produce auto-alert / auto-clear / review actions, learning-to-defer prices escalation against analyst capacity, and alert-queue planning sizes the human budget. It does not implement an override *interface* or record who overrode what, which is a product surface this repository does not have.
- **Article 17 — Quality management system** (unmet): No quality management system exists here, and one cannot: Article 17 is about an organisation's documented procedures, responsibilities and change control, not about code. The CI pipeline and release gate are inputs to such a system, not a substitute for it.
- **Article 43 — Conformity assessment procedure** (unmet): No conformity assessment has been performed. Listing this as a gap is the point: a repository can produce the technical evidence an assessment consumes and cannot perform the assessment on itself.

A quality management system, a risk register with named owners, an override interface that records who overrode what, and a conformity assessment are all things a *company* has, not things a codebase has. Any conformance claim that marked them green from a repository scan would be describing software as though it were an organisation — which is the specific failure mode this document is built to avoid.

## Scope and honest limits

- **This is not a legal assessment and nothing here is legal advice.** Whether this system is
  high-risk under the Act, and which obligations attach to a developer as against a deployer,
  are questions for counsel. The mapping is an engineering inventory written in a regulator's
  vocabulary so that the conversation can start from artifacts rather than from adjectives.
- **The frameworks are pinned and will move.** Both are cited with their versions because a
  control mapped without a version is a control mapped against whatever the author remembered;
  the Act's harmonised standards in particular are still being written.
- **Verification checks existence, not adequacy.** The mechanism proves a named report is
  present, not that it is any good — that judgement is what the reports themselves are for, and
  a reviewer who trusts this table without opening one has learned nothing.
- **A repository cannot be compliant.** An organisation can. Everything marked unmet is
  organisational by nature, and the honest reading of a strong technical column is that the
  *evidence* an assessment would consume exists — not that the assessment has been passed.
